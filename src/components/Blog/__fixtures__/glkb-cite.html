GLKB Cite for Mac: The Papers Behind Any Sentence You Select

GLKB Cite is a small app for your Mac. Select a sentence in your manuscript, click once, and it shows you the PubMed papers that support it, ready to cite. This guide walks you through installing it, setting it up and using it, one step at a time.

At a Glance

You are writing, and you reach a sentence that needs a reference. Normally you would stop, open PubMed, search, read abstracts, and copy the citation by hand. GLKB Cite does that search for you, from inside the app you are already writing in.

It works in three moves.

  1. Select one sentence in Word, Pages, an email, a web page or a PDF.
  2. Click the small blue badge that appears next to your pointer.
  3. Read the papers GLKB found, and copy a reference in the style your journal uses.

Here is what you need before you start.

You need Details
A Mac With macOS 13 Ventura or newer. Both Apple-chip and Intel Macs work.
A GLKB account The same one you use on this website.
A GLKB key A personal code that connects the app to your account. Getting one takes a minute, and this guide shows you how.
About five minutes For the download and the first-time setup.

Not on a Mac?

GLKB Cite is available for Mac only. There is no version for Windows, Linux, iPhone, iPad or Android yet. You can still ask GLKB for the papers behind a claim from any device, in the chat on this website.

Is It for You?

GLKB Cite is useful when you are:

  • writing a manuscript, a grant or a thesis, and a sentence needs a reference
  • reading a draft or a review, and you want to see whether a claim is backed by the literature
  • preparing slides or a report, and you want the source for a statement

It is not a writing tool. It does not write or change your text. It looks at one sentence at a time and brings back papers for you to read and judge.

Check Your Mac

To see which macOS you have, click the Apple logo at the top left of your screen and choose About This Mac. Look at the line that starts with macOS. If the number is 13 or higher, you are ready. If it is lower, GLKB Cite will not open on that Mac.

Get Your GLKB Key

The app needs to know which GLKB account is asking. It does this with a key. A key is a long code that starts with glkb_. Think of it as a password made just for the app. You create it once on this website and paste it into the app once.

  1. Open the GLKB chat page and log in. Log in is at the bottom left of the page.
  2. In the menu on the left, click API. A page called API Dashboard opens.
  3. Click Create key.
  4. Under Key name, type any name that will remind you what the key is for, such as "GLKB Cite on my Mac". Click Next.
  5. The window now says Save your key and shows the key. Click Copy.
  6. Keep that window open until you have pasted the key into the app, or paste it somewhere safe first. The website shows a key only once. After you click Done, it cannot be shown again.

If you lose the key, nothing is broken. Create a new one the same way.

Treat the key like a password

Anyone who has your key can run searches on your account. Do not put it in an email, a shared document or a screenshot.

What a Search Costs

Searches made by the app are paid from the dollar balance on your GLKB account. They do not use the free monthly credits, which are for the chat on this website.

  • Each search is charged what it costs us to run. That is usually a few cents, and rarely more than thirty.
  • You are not charged for a search that fails.
  • You can see what you have left on the API Dashboard page, under Balance.
  • If your balance is empty, the app tells you that GLKB declined the request.

To add to your balance, click your name at the bottom left of the website, choose Account, and look for Add funds under Usage & Balance. If you do not see Add funds, write to us and we will help.

Download and Install

  1. Open the GLKB Cite download page and click Download for Mac. A file called GLKB-Cite-0.2.0.dmg is saved to your Downloads folder. It is small, under 3 MB.
  2. Open your Downloads folder and double-click that file. A window called GLKB Cite Installer opens.
  3. In that window, drag the GLKB Cite icon onto the Applications folder next to it.
  4. Close the window.
  5. Open your Applications folder and double-click GLKB Cite. Open it from there, not from the installer window.
  6. The first time, your Mac may ask whether you are sure you want to open an app downloaded from the internet. Click Open.

The app is signed by the University of Michigan and checked by Apple. Your Mac should open it without any warning about damage or an unknown developer. If you do see such a warning, stop and tell us.

Where the App Lives

GLKB Cite has no main window and no icon in the Dock. This surprises most people the first time. It lives in the menu bar, which is the strip along the top of your screen. Look for a small GLKB icon at the top right, near the clock. Click it to open the app's menu.

First-Time Setup

The first time you open GLKB Cite, a setup window appears. It has four steps and takes about two minutes.

Step 1: Welcome. Read the short overview and click Continue.

Step 2: Allow selected-text access. The app can only help if it can read the sentence you select in another app. Your Mac does not allow that until you say so.

  1. Click Request Access.
  2. Your Mac shows a message. Click Open System Settings.
  3. System Settings opens on Privacy & Security, then Accessibility. Find GLKB Cite in the list and turn its switch on. Your Mac may ask for your password or Touch ID.
  4. Go back to the setup window. It notices the change by itself and says "Access granted".

Apple calls this permission Accessibility. It is how one app is allowed to read text you selected in another. GLKB Cite never reads password boxes or other protected fields.

Step 3: Store your GLKB key. Click in the box and paste your key with Command + V. Click Save, then Continue. The key is kept in your Mac's Keychain, the same protected place where your Mac keeps your saved passwords.

Step 4: Your choices. There are two switches. We suggest leaving both on.

  • Show automatic selection badge. With this on, a small blue badge appears each time you select text.
  • Allow temporary Copy fallback. With this on, the app also works in PDF readers. The PDFs section explains what it does.

Click Finish Setup. The button stays grey until steps 2 and 3 are done.

If your Mac asks about "confidential information"

You may see a message that says GLKB Cite wants to use your confidential information stored in org.glkb.cite. That is your Mac protecting your saved key. Click Always Allow, and it will not ask again.

Find Citations

Select one sentence, the way you would to copy it. Drag across it with the mouse, or double-click and drag. Then ask for citations in any one of these ways.

  • Click the badge. A small blue GLKB badge appears just above and to the right of your pointer. Click it. If you do nothing, the badge goes away after a few seconds. It also goes away when you click somewhere else, type or scroll. Select the sentence again to bring it back.
  • Use the keyboard. Hold down Option and Command, then press G. On the Mac keyboard this is written ⌥⌘G.
  • Use the menu. Click the GLKB icon at the top of your screen and choose Find Citations.

A panel opens at the top right of your screen and says it is searching. A search usually takes 10 to 30 seconds, and now and then up to a minute. GLKB is reading the literature for you during that time. You can keep working while you wait.

Nothing you select is sent anywhere until you click the badge, press the keys or choose Find Citations.

Which Sentences Work

Select one sentence that makes a scientific claim. It should be between about 10 and 1,000 characters long.

Works well Does not work
"Metformin lowers blood glucose mainly by reducing glucose production in the liver." A single word or a gene name alone
A sentence that states a finding, a mechanism or an association A heading, a figure label or a list of keywords
One sentence at a time A whole paragraph or a whole page
A specific claim A vague sentence such as "This has been widely studied."

If the app says the selection cannot be searched, it is usually too short, too long, or more than one sentence.

Read the Results

The papers are numbered and sorted into two groups.

  • Direct evidence. Papers that support the sentence itself. Start here.
  • Indirect context. Papers on closely related findings. They are useful background, but they may not support your exact claim.

For each paper you see its authors, year and journal. Below that is an excerpt, which is the passage from the paper that matched your sentence. You can expand the excerpt to read all of it.

Click a paper's PMID to open it on PubMed in your web browser. The PMID is the ID number PubMed gives every paper.

You can drag the panel anywhere by its top bar. To close it, press Esc or click the ✕.

Always read the paper before you cite it

GLKB Cite is a research aid. It finds candidate papers quickly, and it can be wrong. Open each paper and check that it says what you need it to say. GLKB Cite is not a tool for clinical decisions.

Copy a Citation

Move your pointer over a paper and click Cite. A window opens with the reference written in five styles.

  • MLA 9
  • APA 7
  • Chicago (notes and bibliography)
  • Harvard
  • Vancouver, the numbered style most biomedical journals use

Click the one you want. It is copied. Go back to your document and paste it with Command + V.

If you keep your references in a reference manager, use BibTeX or EndNote in the same window. These are standard formats that programs such as EndNote and Zotero can import.

When the Cite window opens, the app asks PubMed for the paper's volume, issue, pages, DOI and full author names, so that the reference is complete. A line at the bottom of the window tells you whether those details arrived. Without an internet connection the reference is still made, but with the title, authors, journal and year only.

Copy the Whole List

Two buttons at the bottom of the panel copy every paper in the list at once.

  • Copy Plain Text copies the list as simple text. Use it for notes or an email.
  • Copy Rich Report copies the list with its formatting. Use it for a document.

PDFs

Some PDF readers do not let other apps see the text you select. GLKB Cite gets around this in a careful way, and only if Allow temporary Copy fallback is on.

When you click the badge in such an app, GLKB Cite does a quick Copy for you, reads the sentence, and then puts back whatever you had copied before. You will not notice it. It never does this by itself, only when you ask for citations. It also does nothing when the last thing you copied was hidden by a password manager, so a copied password is never touched.

If you use an app that keeps a history of everything you copy, that app may record the sentence for a moment. If that worries you, turn the switch off under Settings, then Privacy.

A scanned PDF is a photograph of a page. It has no text to select, so GLKB Cite cannot read it. Type the sentence into a note and select it there.

Settings

Click the GLKB icon at the top of your screen and choose Settings…. There are three tabs.

Tab What you can change
General Show or hide the badge. Start GLKB Cite when you log in to your Mac, so it is always ready.
Literature Replace or remove your GLKB key. Show or hide the excerpts. Choose how long results are remembered: Off, 5 minutes, 15 minutes or 1 hour.
Privacy Turn the Copy fallback on or off. Read what leaves your Mac.

Remembered results let the panel reopen at once when you ask about the same sentence again. They are kept in memory only and are erased when the app quits.

Your Privacy

  • Nothing is sent until you ask. With the badge on, the app notices when you select text, and reads the selection on your Mac so it can offer the badge. That text stays on your Mac unless you click the badge, press ⌥⌘G or choose Find Citations.
  • What GLKB receives. The one sentence you selected, the number of papers to return, and your key.
  • What PubMed receives. Only the ID numbers of the papers, when you open the Cite window. Never your sentence.
  • Your key stays in your Mac's Keychain.
  • No tracking. The app has no analytics and keeps no history of what you searched.

If Something Goes Wrong

The Badge Does Not Appear

Work down this list.

  1. Click the GLKB icon at the top of your screen. Check that Show Selection Badge has a tick next to it.
  2. If the menu shows Accessibility Access Needed…, choose it. Then turn the switch for GLKB Cite on, as in step 2 of the setup.
  3. If the switch is already on but nothing happens, turn it off and on again.
  4. Select with a drag or a double-click. A single click does not bring up the badge.
  5. In a PDF reader, check that Allow temporary Copy fallback is on under Settings, then Privacy.
  6. Try the keyboard instead. ⌥⌘G often works where the badge cannot.

The Keyboard Shortcut Does Nothing

Another app is using the same keys. Quit that app, then quit and reopen GLKB Cite. Settings, then General, tells you when the shortcut is not available.

"GLKB rejected this API key"

The key saved in the app is switched off, deleted or mistyped. Create a new key on the website, as in Get Your GLKB Key. Then open Settings, then Literature, paste the new key and click Save. Paste it as one line, with nothing before or after it.

"GLKB declined this request"

Your account has reached a limit. Most often the balance is empty, or too many searches were sent at once. Wait a moment and try again. If it keeps happening, check your balance as described in What a Search Costs.

"No citation result is available yet"

GLKB found no papers for that sentence. Try a more specific sentence that states one clear claim.

The Panel Opened Somewhere Unexpected

The panel opens at the top right of the screen you are working on. If you dragged it, it stays where you put it until you close it. Press Esc and search again to send it back to the corner.

Update or Remove

To update, first quit GLKB Cite from its menu at the top of your screen. Then download the newest version from the download page and install it the same way. When your Mac asks, choose to replace the old copy. Your settings and your key are kept.

To remove the app, open Settings, then Literature, and click Remove to delete your key from the Mac. Then quit GLKB Cite and drag it from your Applications folder to the Trash.
